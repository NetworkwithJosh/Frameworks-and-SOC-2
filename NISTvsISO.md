
# NIST CSF 2.0 vs. ISO/IEC 27001:2022

## Overview

NIST CSF 2.0 and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 are both used to help organizations manage cybersecurity and information security risks. However, they approach risk management differently.

NIST CSF 2.0 provides a flexible, outcome-based framework for assessing and improving cybersecurity practices.

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 establishes formal requirements for an Information Security Management System (ISMS). Organizations can also pursue independent certification against the standard.

The purpose of this comparison is to understand how these two approaches differ, where they overlap, and how organizations can use them together.

## Comparison Chart

| Category | NIST CSF 2.0 | ISO/IEC 27001:2022 |
|---|---|---|
| **Classification** | Cybersecurity framework | International information security management standard |
| **Primary Purpose** | Helps organizations understand, assess, prioritize, and manage cybersecurity risks | Establishes requirements for implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an ISMS |
| **Geographic Use** | Developed in the United States but used globally | Internationally recognized and used globally |
| **Approach** | Flexible and outcome-based | Requirements-based, with flexibility in implementation |
| **Structure** | Six Core Functions, Categories, Subcategories, Profiles, and Tiers | Clauses 4–10 and Annex A controls |
| **Risk Management** | Helps organizations assess cybersecurity risks and prioritize improvements | Requires a defined information security risk assessment and treatment process |
| **Security Controls** | Organizations select controls and practices to achieve relevant cybersecurity outcomes | Annex A provides 93 reference controls across four categories |
| **Documentation** | Depends on organizational needs and applicable obligations | Requires specified documented information to demonstrate ISMS conformity |
| **Certification** | No official NIST CSF certification offered by NIST | Independent ISO/IEC 27001 certification is available |
| **Assessment** | Gap assessments, Current and Target Profiles, and outcome evaluations | Internal audits, management reviews, and optional external certification audits |
| **Evidence** | Security configurations, logs, policies, assessment results, and operational records | ISMS documentation, risk assessments, Statement of Applicability, audit records, and control evidence |
| **Continuous Improvement** | Organizations can use Current and Target Profiles to identify and prioritize improvements | Continual improvement of the ISMS is a formal requirement |
| **Best Use Case** | Organizations looking to understand and improve their cybersecurity posture | Organizations establishing a formal and auditable ISMS |

## NIST CSF 2.0: Six Core Functions

1. **Govern (GV):** Establish cybersecurity risk management strategy, policies, roles, and oversight.
2. **Identify (ID):** Understand organizational assets and cybersecurity risks.
3. **Protect (PR):** Implement safeguards to manage cybersecurity risks.
4. **Detect (DE):** Identify and analyze possible cybersecurity attacks and compromises.
5. **Respond (RS):** Take action regarding detected cybersecurity incidents.
6. **Recover (RC):** Restore affected assets and operations following cybersecurity incidents.

## ISO/IEC 27001:2022: Annex A Control Categories

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 contains 93 Annex A controls organized into four categories.

| Control Category | Number of Controls |
|---|---:|
| Organizational Controls | 37 |
| People Controls | 8 |
| Physical Controls | 14 |
| Technological Controls | 34 |
| **Total** | **93** |

The organization determines which controls are necessary based on its risk assessment, risk treatment decisions, and applicable requirements. These decisions are documented in the Statement of Applicability (SoA).

## Practical Example: Employee Access Management

**Scenario:** An employee leaves an organization, but their Microsoft 365 account remains active because HR never notified IT.

### NIST CSF 2.0 Perspective

The organization can evaluate relevant cybersecurity outcomes associated with identity management, access control, responsibilities, and risk management.

The assessment can help identify gaps in the offboarding process and prioritize improvements.

### ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Perspective

The organization evaluates its established access management and offboarding processes against applicable ISMS requirements and selected controls.

Relevant Annex A controls may include:

- **A.5.18:** Access rights
- **A.6.5:** Responsibilities after termination or change of employment
- **A.8.2:** Privileged access rights, where applicable

The organization should evaluate why access was not revoked and determine appropriate corrective actions.

### Examples of Supporting Evidence

- Employee termination records
- HR-to-IT offboarding notifications
- User account status reports
- Access revocation tickets
- Quarterly access review records
- Evidence of remediation and management approval

## Can Organizations Use Both?

Yes. NIST CSF 2.0 and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 are not mutually exclusive.

An organization can use ISO/IEC 27001 to establish a formal information security management system while using NIST CSF 2.0 to assess cybersecurity outcomes, identify gaps, and prioritize security improvements.

Using both can provide a structured management system alongside a flexible approach to evaluating cybersecurity risk.

## Key Takeaways

- NIST CSF 2.0 is a framework, while ISO/IEC 27001:2022 is a requirements-based standard.
- Both can be used by organizations anywhere in the world.
- NIST CSF focuses on cybersecurity outcomes and risk management.
- ISO/IEC 27001 establishes requirements for an Information Security Management System.
- ISO/IEC 27001 certification provides independent assurance of ISMS conformity within the certified scope, not a guarantee of security.
- Both approaches require organizations to understand risks and evaluate whether their security practices are effective.
- Organizations can use both together to strengthen their security governance and risk management practices.
