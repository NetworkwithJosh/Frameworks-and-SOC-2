
# Module 3: Framework and Assurance Comparison

## Overview

This project compares four cybersecurity frameworks, standards, and assurance approaches used in cybersecurity controls consulting, IT auditing, and Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC).

The comparison focuses on purpose, type, audience, scope, and assessment approach.

## Framework Comparison

| Category | NIST CSF 2.0 | ISO/IEC 27001 | SOC 2 | NIST SP 800-53 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Manage and prioritize cybersecurity risks. | Establish and improve an Information Security Management System (ISMS). | Provide independent assurance about relevant service organization controls. | Provide detailed security and privacy controls. |
| Type | Voluntary cybersecurity risk management framework. | International certifiable management-system standard. | Independent CPA attestation examination. | Security and privacy control catalog. |
| Audience | CISOs, executives, risk managers, engineers, and consultants. | Management, security teams, employees, and auditors. | Service organizations, customers, auditors, and vendor risk teams. | Federal agencies, contractors, security engineers, and assessors. |
| Scope | Flexible and risk-based, covering relevant organizational cybersecurity risks. | Defined ISMS boundaries, including people, processes, systems, and dependencies. | Defined service system, commitments, applicable Trust Services Criteria, and supporting controls. | Selected organizational and system controls based on risk and requirements. |
| Assessment Approach | Gap assessments, Current and Target Profiles, and risk-based improvement planning. | Internal audits, management reviews, corrective actions, and certification audits. | Type I assesses control design at a date; Type II also assesses operating effectiveness over a period. | Examine evidence, interview personnel, and test technical and procedural controls. |

## Key Differences

### NIST CSF 2.0
- Focuses on cybersecurity risk management.
- Provides flexible outcomes organizations can tailor.
- Does not have an official certification scheme equivalent to ISO 27001.

### ISO/IEC 27001
- Establishes requirements for an ISMS.
- Supports accredited third-party certification.
- Emphasizes risk management and continual improvement.

### SOC 2
- Provides independent assurance through a CPA attestation report.
- Uses applicable Trust Services Criteria.
- Type II examines operating effectiveness over a defined period.
- Is not a certification.

### NIST SP 800-53
- Provides detailed security and privacy controls.
- Supports security control selection and assessment.
- Helps connect governance objectives with technical safeguards.

## Conclusion

These four approaches serve complementary purposes.

NIST CSF 2.0 helps organizations organize cybersecurity risk management.

ISO/IEC 27001 establishes a structured information security management system.

SOC 2 provides independent assurance about controls relevant to service commitments.

NIST SP 800-53 provides detailed security and privacy controls that can be selected, implemented, and tested.

**Key takeaway:** Mapping a control across multiple frameworks does not automatically demonstrate compliance. Each requirement must be evaluated against its objectives, scope, and evidence expectations.
