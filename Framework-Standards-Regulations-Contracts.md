Framework, Standards, Regulations, and Contracts

A framework organizes outcomes, practices or concepts. It may be flexible and nonprescriptive.

A Standard establishes requirements requirements or specifications than an organization may adopt or be assessed against.

A regulation creates a legally enforceable obligation within its jurisdiction.

A contract creates obligations between the parties that agreed to it.

A policy is an internal statement of management direction and expectations 

A control is the action used to address risk or meet an objective 

The same organization may be affected by all six at once. Your job is to understand what creates the requirement, what is in scope, and what evidence demonstrates compliance.

So the question is, if we know know what they are how do we classify one 

Assignment: Classify five examples as framework, standard, regulation,contract, policy or control 

Frameworks:
1. The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 is a comprehensive, flexible, and sector‑agnostic framework designed to help organizations understand, assess, prioritize, and communicate cybersecurity risks.
2.  NIST Risk Management Framework - a structured process for managing security and privacy risk
3.  NIST AI Risk Management Framework — guidance for trustworthy AI systems.
4.  A SWOT analysis helps organizations understand their internal strengths and weaknesses and their external opportunities and threats
5.  The Balanced Scorecard (BSC) is a strategic management framework that aligns an organization’s day‑to‑day activities with its long‑term goals by measuring performance across four perspectives: Financial, Customer, Internal Process, and Learning & Growth.

Standards 
1. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 is the internationally recognized standard for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Information Security Management System (ISMS)
2. ISO/IEC 42001 is the international standard for establishing, implementing, and improving Artificial Intelligence Management Systems (AIMS) to ensure responsible, ethical, and transparent AI practices.
3. ISO 22301 is requirements for Business Continuity Management Systems to ensure resilience against disruptions.
4. COBIT is a governance framework for IT, including cybersecurity, aligned with business goals
5. PCI DSS was developed to encourage and enhance payment card account data security and facilitate the broad adoption of consistent data security measures globally. PCI DSS provides a baseline of technical and operational requirements designed to protect payment account data.

Regulations 
1. General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) — EU Regulation 2016/679
2. EU Artificial Intelligence Act (EU AI Act) — comprehensive EU AI governance framework
3. Sarbanes–Oxley Act (SOX) — U.S. corporate accountability and financial controls law
4. Ireland’s Data Protection Act 2018 — national law giving effect to GDPR in Ireland
5. ePrivacy Regulations refer to the EU’s legal framework governing the confidentiality of electronic communications, currently based on the ePrivacy Directive, since the proposed ePrivacy Regulation was withdrawn in 2025 and never adopted.

Contracts 
1. A Statement of Work is a project‑specific contract that defines the exact work a vendor will perform—scope, deliverables, timelines, milestones, and acceptance criteria .
2. A vendor contract is the overarching agreement governing the entire business relationship with an external supplier—services, pricing, liability, confidentiality, security, and termination
3. Service Order Agreement—a document that authorizes or itemizes a specific service purchase under a broader contract (similar to an Order Form). In some compliance contexts, SOA may also refer to a Service Organization Agreement (e.g., SOC reports), but in vendor contracting it usually means the former.
4. An SLA is legally considered a contract when it meets contract‑law requirements, and most SLAs function either as standalone contracts or as contractual components within a broader agreement.
5. A Microsoft Services Agreement (MSA) is the contract that governs your use of Microsoft’s consumer online products and services, including Microsoft accounts, Outlook.com, OneDrive, and other consumer-facing tools.
